# Natural copy transitions

Use this reference when product or marketing copy feels choppy, over-signposted, or AI-written. Good transitions help readers scan a React page, understand section order, and keep moving toward the next action.

Transitions are not filler. If the layout, heading, or visual hierarchy already carries the transition, do not add a sentence just to sound smooth.

## Where transitions help in web UI

- landing-page sections that need a clearer narrative flow
- long-form product pages or docs-like marketing pages
- feature pages moving from problem to outcome to proof
- pricing pages moving from plan choice to objections
- onboarding or setup copy where each step depends on the previous one

## Practical transition patterns

Use plain phrases that match the reader's next question.

| Job | Natural patterns |
| --- | --- |
| preview structure | `Here's what matters first:`, `The flow is simple:`, `Start here:` |
| introduce next section | `Next, show...`, `Now explain...`, `Then prove...` |
| add supporting point | `Also,`, `Plus,`, `Another reason:` |
| contrast | `But`, `That said,`, `Unlike {alternative},` |
| cause and effect | `So`, `That means`, `That's why`, `The result:` |
| example | `For example:`, `Take {scenario}:`, `Say a user wants to...` |
| key point | `The main point:`, `What matters:`, `Bottom line:` |
| summary | `Short version:`, `To recap:`, `The takeaway:` |
| CTA bridge | `Ready to {outcome}?`, `Start with {low-friction step}.`, `See what changes first.` |

For React pages, this usually means writing clearer section headers and short intro lines rather than inserting transition paragraphs between every component.

## Question-based transitions

Questions work when they match what the reader is already thinking:

- `Not sure which plan fits?`
- `Need proof before switching?`
- `Worried about setup time?`
- `What happens after you sign up?`
- `How does this work with your current stack?`

Do not use questions to fake empathy. If the question is not a real objection or curiosity point, cut it.

## Evidence transitions

When making claims, use attribution that tells users where confidence comes from:

- `Customer interviews show...`
- `In support tickets, teams often mention...`
- `A case study with {company} found...`
- `{customer} reported...`
- `The benchmark shows...`

If no proof exists, soften the claim or remove it. Do not manufacture authority with vague phrases like `research shows` when no source is available.

## AI-tell phrases to avoid

These usually make product copy sound generated:

- `That being said`
- `It's worth noting that`
- `At its core`
- `In today's digital landscape`
- `When it comes to the realm of`
- `Let's delve into`
- `This begs the question`
- `Unlock the power of`
- `Seamlessly transform`
- `Revolutionize your workflow`

Replace them with the actual point.

Weak:

`In today's digital landscape, teams need seamless collaboration.`

Stronger:

`When planning moves from Slack to spreadsheets, decisions get lost.`

## Best practices

- front-load the important noun or action
- vary transition patterns so the page does not sound templated
- use transitions at section boundaries, not every paragraph
- match tone to audience: B2B can be direct and calm; consumer pages can be more conversational
- read the page aloud and cut any transition that sounds ceremonial
- keep transitions shorter on narrow layouts where copy competes with controls and visuals

## Review checklist

- each section follows logically from the previous one
- headings carry enough meaning for scanners
- transition copy answers a real reader question
- no paragraph starts with a generic AI-tell phrase
- examples and proof sit near the claims they support
- CTA bridge explains what happens next, not just why the product is good
